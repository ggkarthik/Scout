## 1. Summary

Deliver dependable discovery across contracted AWS, Azure and Copilot Studio resources, and make observation gaps actionable. Reuse a common reference-comparison and health workflow while proving each provider's completeness independently. Select product resource types based on the intended controls; SageMaker may validate the method but cannot alone qualify unrelated AWS surfaces.

## 3. Background

The [roadmap](ai-grid-prioritized-roadmap-2026-2029.md) prioritizes reliable observation before approval comparisons, control qualification and remediation. Existing collectors do not establish complete discovery or customer acceptance.

Confirmed repository foundations and limitations:

- AWS `AwsBedrockDiscoveryService.collectSageMaker` paginates endpoint/configuration enumeration but never calls `ListModels` or `DescribeModel`. Unreferenced models are structurally undiscoverable through this path. Synthesized `AWS_SAGEMAKER_MODEL_REFERENCE` ARNs with `referenceOnly: true` can equal real model ARNs without proving existence; a plain ARN join must not count them as verified models.
- The same AWS method shares one exception boundary across ten families. Early `ListDomains` denial blocks later endpoint/configuration collection. Its catch appends failed payloads even for completed families; the discovery loop keeps the first scope payload and discards duplicates. The issue is blocked collection and discarded diagnostics, not competing persisted rows.
- AWS has four hardcoded `arn:aws:sagemaker:` constructions and four single-page List calls for training, processing, transform jobs and pipelines. Noncommercial partition support and these excluded types' full enumeration remain known deferred work; their failures must be isolated from contracted collection.
- `AzureAiDiscoveryService` has ARM and Foundry collection paths, capability flags and scope diagnostics. Qualification must distinguish management-plane resources from data-plane children, disabled capabilities and new/classic Foundry APIs; no Azure reliability claim is established here.
- `CopilotStudioDiscoveryService` uses Dataverse bots/components and creates version artifacts from bot row metadata, with a fallback label of `published`. `CopilotStudioDataverseClient` follows next links but filters its current queries by component state. These paths require independent lifecycle/population checks; generated version labels do not prove deployed-version identity.
- `AiGridCoverageService` projects latest complete scope heads. Existing policy/evidence coverage is not an independent discovery denominator. The AWS connector UI shows run/scope diagnostics and missing permissions; the full reference-derived missing-resource queue is new work.

The [AWS Bedrock Agents documentation](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/cli/latest/reference/bedrock-agent/) states Classic agents are unavailable to new customers. Keep Classic out of fresh-account defaults; this does not justify excluding other AWS product surfaces. Azure Resource Graph has explicit [paging requirements](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/resource-graph/concepts/paging-results). Copilot [solution exports](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-copilot-studio/authoring-solutions-import-export) can omit components unless included, so a selected solution export alone cannot establish environment completeness.

## 4. Objective and success measures

1. Archive independently reviewed unchanged-collector baselines for every contracted provider and slice before fixes.
2. Reach 100% stable-fixture discovery and ≥95% independent-reference coverage per provider and non-empty slice, with every discrepancy explained. No provider aggregate can hide a failing provider or slice.
3. Complete each provider's 14-day window. Ordinary complete runs meet test criteria; intentional faults remain labelled failed/incomplete and recover within 24 hours after fault removal. Preserve every scheduled attempt and explain schedule gaps.
4. Let an operator locate a gap, identify its impact, assign an owner, follow the next action and confirm closure through fresh evidence.
5. Qualify the selected existing controls only after their discovery/evidence prerequisites pass. Policy qualification does not replace discovery acceptance, or vice versa.

## 5. Users and jobs

Engineering operators need to find and fix collector gaps. Platform owners need to resolve access and setup issues without reading backend logs. Independent reviewers need to reproduce coverage claims. Customer platform/security teams need to understand which conclusions remain blocked; they enter qualification only after onboarding passes.

## 6. Value proposition

Answer: **What are we missing, why does it matter, and who needs to fix it?** A resource count alone cannot answer this. Show trustworthy observations alongside explicit blind spots and their next actions, without treating missing evidence as a clean security result.

## 7. Solution

### 7.1 Shared reference and comparison contract

Each provider manifest records exact boundaries, immutable identity rules, reference/collector principal permissions, shared blind spots, pinned tools/API versions, requests/filters, start/end times, pagination completion, exclusions and artifact hashes. Use an independently authorized read-only reference principal; different credentials alone do not establish independence or sufficient permissions.

Run reference enumeration immediately before and after the collector within a proposed 30-minute interval. Freeze ordinary fixtures. Preserve both populations when churn occurs and rerun the stable comparison; intentional mutation cases are separate tests. Reference denial, truncation or unverified blind spots invalidate qualification. Do not shrink a denominator to mask a failure.

Archive sanitized List IDs and completion evidence. AWS operations require all token pages; Azure queries/child APIs require all continuation pages; Copilot must follow all qualified Dataverse continuations. Verify instruments against pinned versions and known fixtures. Document any API incapable of enumerating a contracted type; hold that type's qualification.

Match immutable IDs within provider/tenant/account/subscription/environment/region/type. Use provider ARNs for AWS, source IDs for Azure, and environment-scoped entity GUIDs for Copilot. Record provider-specific normalization rules independently. Names, synthesized references and guessed versions do not establish resource existence. Prevent duplicates and cross-environment/name collisions.

`Discovery coverage = unique fresh verified resources matched to independent reference / unique supported in-scope reference resources × 100`.

Keep the known stable-fixture completion metric alongside this percentage. Unexpected supported reference resources still belong in the denominator. Explained missing resources stay missing. Empty slices are N/A. A collector failure leaves the reference population intact but cannot qualify that run; prior successful observations remain historical evidence with their original timestamps.

Retain only allowlisted identity/configuration metadata, timestamps, statuses and relationships. Exclude credentials, container environment values, prompts/responses, component content, conversations, tool payloads and model/data contents from reference exports, snapshots, logs and UI. If a policy needs prohibited content, defer or redesign its evidence contract.

### 7.2 UI locations and actionable gaps

Add a shared **Discovery health** view reachable from AI inventory and each provider connector. Show provider, contracted scope, reference qualification/time, reference/observed/matched counts, freshness, last attempt, last complete observation and failures. Display Discovery coverage separately from Policy coverage. If no qualified reference exists, show coverage unverified; never infer it from observed counts.

Add a **Coverage gaps** queue in that view. Reference-only resources appear here even though they have no normal inventory record; show sanitized provider identity and reference evidence. Each item includes provider/scope/type, category, first/last seen, source run/comparison, explanation, affected controls or impact-unknown state, owner, next action, status and verification evidence. Keep inaccessible, unsupported, disabled and outside-boundary labels distinct.

| Gap | Action and owner |
|---|---|
| Missing permission | Platform owner reviews the exact denied operation/required permission, corrects access, tests and rescans. Do not grant broader permissions automatically. |
| Reference-only resource | Engineering owner investigates enumeration, identity and filtering; fixes the demonstrated defect and reruns comparison. |
| Throttled/incomplete scan | Operator retries after the reported limit clears; repeated failure routes to engineering with correlation evidence. |
| Stale evidence | Operator runs discovery and investigates scheduling/access failures. |
| Unsupported type | Product owner accepts a disclosed limitation or plans supported discovery with a new reference contract. |
| Collector-only/possible deletion | Operator checks existence/churn; current inventory changes only after complete authoritative enumeration confirms absence. |

Gap statuses: Open → Assigned → In progress → Awaiting verification → Verified resolved. A reviewer may record an accepted limitation, but it remains visible and cannot count as a match or satisfy a missing-fixture requirement. Repeated comparison results update the same scoped gap; recur/reopen when fresh evidence shows failure again. Owner/action changes are audited. Ticket closure or clicking Resolve cannot substitute for fresh verification.

Reuse Test and Run discovery actions where supported; make pending/result/failure visible and respect connector authorization. No automatic permission changes, resource deletion or security remediation. Discovery defects and unavailable evidence are operational gaps; create a security finding only when a qualified policy has evidence of a security failure. Downstream views must link blocking gaps and retain UNKNOWN for missing/stale evidence.

### 7.3 Required acceptance scenarios — every provider

| Scenario | Required result |
|---|---|
| Normal/empty | All stable fixtures discovered; same names across scopes remain distinct. Complete empty results are N/A, not failed or 100%. |
| Pagination | Multiple real pages; no dropped/duplicated IDs. Interrupted or repeated tokens cannot produce completion or infinite loops. |
| Permissions | Denied List, denied detail and credential failure identify operation/scope. Accessible slices continue; partial scans cannot establish deletion. Reference denial invalidates comparison. |
| Throttling | Respect provider retry hints where present; bounded backoff with jitter. Proposed five attempts per call and ten-minute slice deadline; exhaustion remains incomplete. Local budgets/admission limits are visible. |
| Freshness | Evidence older than 24 hours becomes stale without needing another scan. Failed attempts do not refresh last-complete time. |
| Missing/deletion/churn | Reference-only resources remain discrepancies. Confirm absence only through later complete enumeration of the exact slice; retain history. Preserve time-window differences. |
| Provider isolation | Failure in AWS cannot qualify or invalidate Azure/Copilot results implicitly; unhealthy providers/slices remain visible and block their dependent controls. |
| UI action/closure | An operator assigns a reference-only gap, follows its next action and sees closure only after a new successful comparison. Diagnostics are accessible, not limited to the first error. Recurrence reopens the gap. |
| Privacy/isolation | Synthetic sensitive values and cross-tenant/environment data never appear outside allowed boundaries. |

Add provider-specific cases: AWS unreferenced models and early/late unrelated-family denial; Azure ARM-versus-child completeness, feature flags and API-version mismatch; Copilot lifecycle filters, environment identity, component categories and version-authority checks. Use live separate test scopes for completeness, real pagination and deletion, plus labelled fault injection for deterministic errors. Simulated tests alone cannot qualify live coverage.

### 7.4 Control-to-resource mapping and bounded policy qualification

Before approving product resource types, map the intended **5–8 controls across the agreed workflow**, not automatically 5–8 per provider. Each row records policy ID/version/provider, root and linked types, relationships, required facts/API fields, permissions, freshness, independent reference, positive/benign/missing/stale/denied cases and owner. Inspect predicates and fact-producer paths; package counts and GA status do not prove evaluability.

Candidate categories: AWS endpoint lifecycle, AgentCore authorization/target configuration, guardrails and qualified linked resources; Azure Foundry configuration and qualified identity/network/data relationships; Copilot Studio configuration and component metadata. These are selection inputs, not approved policies or claims that all evidence is available. Prefer existing packages. Add a new policy only for a documented valuable gap with an authoritative evidence contract and separate review; revise the approved scope/estimate first.

Policy qualification starts after the relevant provider/type discovery passes. Missing, stale or denied required evidence yields UNKNOWN, never a clean pass. Require the normal independent-review and production-release precision gates from the roadmap; demo/dev approvals cannot qualify customer controls. Keep discovery-health checks operational, separate from security policy findings. This milestone supports rank 3 without absorbing its full control-pack release into rank 1.

If SageMaker is method-only, record its owner/capacity and a subsequent AWS product-boundary increment before declaring AWS discovery qualified for the intended workflow. Every additional type needs reference qualification. A failing provider blocks its dependent controls; separately qualified provider releases may proceed transparently. The combined three-provider release passes only when all contracted boundaries pass.

### 7.5 Constraints and customer prerequisites

Exclude universal provider coverage, AWS Organizations-wide discovery, sovereign partitions/clouds, uncontracted types/regions/environments, Microsoft 365/GitHub Copilot products outside Copilot Studio, raw runtime/content collection, inline enforcement, approval-registry implementation and automatic remediation. Existing unsupported/unqualified collector output must not inherit acceptance from another type.

Secure onboarding evidence must pass before customer collection or qualification: customer IdP/roles/revocation, tenant-isolation negative tests, least-privilege permissions, secrets/support access, metadata minimization, agreed regions/retention and audit. Include roadmap production checks for demo-data restrictions, dev-approval bypasses and intended authentication/tenant behavior. Failure keeps customer collection blocked; separate test work may continue. Production operations remain a separate release gate.

Resolve named owners, exact IDs/endpoints, API availability, licenses/quotas, retention and time/cost limits before live runs. Twelve-hour cadence over 14 days gives 28 rounds and 56 before/after reference batches **per provider**. With all three windows, that is 168 provider batches before retries and reruns; request volume depends on slices/pages/detail calls. The proposed AWS SageMaker baseline alone entails 672 List traversals. Re-estimate Azure/Copilot and expanded AWS types separately; do not reuse the one-provider historical 3–5 engineer-week range as a three-provider commitment.

## 8. Release and first development task

Work under one three-provider PRD with separate provider milestones. For the assumed small team, reuse a common contract and deliver sequentially by default; scope expansion does not create extra engineering capacity. Decide provider order from access and control mapping. Dates, owners and budget remain to be agreed.

1. **Planning gate:** approve mappings and separate manifests; disclose method-only work and unresolved reference limitations. This is specification work before implementation.
2. **D1 — Independent exports and unchanged-collector baselines:** implement provider-specific exporters and a shared comparator/report without collector enumeration/normalization reuse. Preserve sanitized reference evidence, run/build IDs, slice counts and discrepancies. Quantify the known AWS gaps and establish Azure/Copilot baselines independently. Done when another engineer can reproduce each report and the reviewer accepts its denominator. No coverage-health redesign precedes this baseline.
3. **D2 — Collector fixes:** AWS direct model enumeration, reference-only provenance and failure isolation; Azure/Copilot fixes tied to their baselines and evidence-contract checks. Preserve before/after comparisons and meaningful regression evidence. Excluded partition/type expansion remains explicit.
4. **D3 — Actionable UI and discovery qualification:** deliver shared health/queue workflows, pass scenarios and each provider's 14-day window. Archive manifests, baselines/final reports, reviewer dispositions and independent provider release decisions. A reference CSV alone does not satisfy UI acceptance.
5. **D4 — Bounded policy qualification:** qualify selected existing controls after their observation/evidence prerequisites pass; preserve individual discovery and policy outcomes. Hold unsupported controls rather than widening claims.
6. **Customer qualification:** pass onboarding first, then agree customer manifests and repeat per-provider reference qualification, ≥95% per-provider/per-slice coverage, discrepancy review and control qualification. Test-account results are not customer acceptance.

Hold the affected qualification if the reference is indefensible, blind spots remain unresolved, targets fail, discrepancies lack explanations or budget is exhausted. Record remediate, rescope, defer or stop with owner and review date. Preserve prior manifests/failures. Completion of all three contracted provider boundaries is required for the combined deliverable; passing one does not imply the others pass.
