# ScoutGrid: Solo-Founder Go-to-Market Plan

**Planning horizon:** 90 days  
**Operating assumption:** one person owns product marketing, outbound, demos, pilots, partners, content, customer success, and measurement.  
**Primary objective:** create a repeatable path from a tightly defined security problem to qualified pipeline, paid pilots, and the first referenceable customers.

## 1. Strategic choice

ScoutGrid should enter the market through one narrow promise:

> **ScoutGrid gives security and cloud teams an evidence-backed inventory of their AI systems and the highest-priority posture and exposure risks across AWS and Azure—without requiring access to prompts, model content, or production data.**

Use the broader SBOM/vulnerability capability as an expansion path, not the first message:

1. AI inventory and posture visibility.
2. Evidence-backed prioritization and ownership of AI findings.
3. Cross-system exposure correlation.
4. SBOM, vulnerability, and remediation workflow expansion.

### Initial ideal customer profile

Prioritize companies that:

- have active AI workloads in AWS Bedrock, SageMaker, Azure AI Foundry, Azure ML, or connected data/tool systems;
- have a security, cloud-security, AppSec, or GRC owner but no dedicated AI-security platform;
- need an inventory and evidence trail for internal risk reviews, customer questionnaires, audits, or board reporting;
- are roughly 200–5,000 employees, where the problem is real but buying cycles are still approachable;
- can approve a read-only cloud connector and participate in a 2–4 week pilot.

### Buyer and champion

- **Economic buyer:** CISO, VP Security, or Head of Cloud Security.
- **Champion:** Cloud Security Architect, Application Security Lead, AI Platform Lead, or Security Engineering Manager.
- **Influencers:** GRC/compliance, platform engineering, data/ML engineering, procurement.

### Accounts to deprioritize for now

- very small companies with no cloud-security owner;
- large enterprises requiring a year-long procurement, broad SSO/RBAC customization, or many-region deployment before a pilot;
- buyers asking primarily for generic vulnerability scanning, unless the AI-security wedge is also relevant;
- prospects requiring ScoutGrid to claim certification, complete framework coverage, or runtime protection that the product does not provide.

## 3. Phase 1 — Days 1–30: sharpen, validate, and make the motion sellable

### Objectives

- Select one beachhead segment and one primary use case.
- Turn the product into a crisp demo and pilot.
- Build the first account list and learn the buyer's language.
- Start conversations before spending time on broad marketing.

### Week 1: positioning and readiness

- Write a one-page positioning brief: ICP, trigger events, pains, current alternatives, differentiators, proof, and disqualifiers.
- Create three message variants for testing:
  - “Find every AI system and owner across AWS/Azure.”
  - “Turn AI-security posture gaps into evidence-backed, prioritized findings.”
  - “See AI exposure and supply-chain risk without ingesting prompts or sensitive data.”
- Build a 12-minute demo: discover inventory → show evidence → show priority finding → show owner/workflow → show executive output.
- Define the pilot as a fixed outcome, not an open-ended implementation:
  - read-only connector;
  - one cloud/provider and one business scope;
  - inventory, posture findings, coverage gaps, and prioritized remediation review;
  - written findings/readout at the end.
- Prepare a security/procurement pack: architecture, data handling, permissions, deployment model, subprocessors, support expectations, and FAQ.
- Confirm all public claims are accurate. Say “mapped to” or “informed by” frameworks where appropriate; do not imply certification or complete coverage.

### Week 2: list building and customer discovery

- Build a list of 100 accounts using public signals: AI job postings, Bedrock/Azure AI usage, AI governance announcements, cloud-security hiring, recent compliance activity, and visible AI products.
- Identify 1–2 contacts per account: security leader plus technical champion.
- Conduct 10 problem interviews. Do not lead with a product demo; learn:
  - how AI assets are inventoried today;
  - what evidence is needed for risk decisions;
  - who owns remediation;
  - what triggers budget and urgency;
  - what would make a pilot worthwhile.
- Ask every interviewee for one introduction to a peer or cloud/security partner.
- Start a simple CRM with stages: Target → Contacted → Replied → Discovery → Qualified → Pilot → Proposal → Won/Lost.

### Week 3: outbound and first design partners

- Send 15–20 highly personalized outbound messages per weekday, in two small account batches.
- Use a short sequence over 10–12 business days: insight → relevant question → proof/demo offer → breakup message.
- Offer a “20-minute AI exposure baseline” or “AI inventory readiness review,” not a generic demo.
- Recruit 2 design partners. Selection criteria:
  - clear AI/cloud scope;
  - named technical owner;
  - read-only access possible;
  - executive or customer deadline within 60 days;
  - willingness to provide feedback and a testimonial if successful.
- Run 3–5 live demos and record objections verbatim.

### Week 4: pilot packaging and public foundation

- Run the first pilot(s) with a written success plan and a scheduled executive readout.
- Launch a focused landing page with: problem, audience, 3 outcomes, architecture/data boundary, pilot CTA, and a short FAQ.
- Publish two pieces of practical content:
  - “AI security inventory checklist for AWS and Azure.”
  - “What read-only AI posture evidence can and cannot prove.”
- Create one proof asset from the pilot baseline, even if anonymized.
- Review the funnel. Kill the message/segment with weak response and double down on the strongest trigger.

### Day-30 exit criteria

- One primary ICP and one primary use case selected.
- 20 buyer/problem conversations completed or scheduled.
- At least 8 qualified discovery calls and 2 design partners.
- Demo, pilot scope, security pack, pricing hypothesis, CRM, and follow-up templates are usable.
- One message earns materially better replies than the others.

## 4. Phase 2 — Days 31–60: convert learning into pipeline and paid pilots

### Objectives

- Convert design partners into paid pilots or strong commercial proposals.
- Establish two repeatable acquisition channels.
- Build partner-sourced opportunities without creating a heavy channel program.
- Publish evidence that reduces buyer risk.

### Sales execution

- Maintain a weekly target of 30–40 personalized touches, 4–6 discovery calls, and 2–3 demos.
- Score opportunities on pain, urgency, access, champion, technical fit, and buying path. Disqualify politely when three or more are missing.
- Use a mutual action plan for every qualified pilot: problem, scope, access owner, dates, success metrics, security review, readout, and commercial decision.
- Make every pilot produce a quantified before/after result, such as:
  - AI assets discovered;
  - unknown or ownerless assets found;
  - high-priority posture gaps validated;
  - time reduced to prepare an AI-risk review;
  - findings routed to responsible teams.
- Ask for the commercial decision before the final readout: “If we achieve these outcomes, what would the next step and approval path be?”

### Marketing system

Use a low-volume, high-relevance cadence:

- one technical insight post per week;
- one deeper article or teardown every two weeks;
- one monthly live session or small roundtable for security/cloud practitioners;
- one customer/pilot proof asset as soon as permission allows.

Content themes:

- AI asset inventory and ownership;
- read-only evidence and data minimization;
- AI exposure paths across agents, tools, knowledge stores, and sensitive data;
- how AI posture connects to SBOM and vulnerability remediation;
- practical preparation for customer security reviews.

Repurpose every customer conversation into anonymized FAQs, objections, content ideas, and product improvements. Do not start SEO as a broad program; target a small set of high-intent phrases and pages.

### Partner motion

Approach partners who already have trust and access to the ICP:

- cloud-security consultancies and AWS/Azure specialists;
- vCISOs and boutique security advisory firms;
- penetration-testing and AppSec firms that need AI posture visibility;
- MSP/MSSP teams serving mid-market cloud customers;
- SBOM, GRC, DevSecOps, and AI-governance consultants.

Partner offer:

- a joint AI-security baseline for their customer;
- referral fee or sourced-opportunity economics only after a qualified opportunity;
- co-branded workshop/demo;
- clear boundaries: ScoutGrid provides product and technical support; partner owns advisory/service delivery.

Have 10 partner conversations, recruit 4 active partners, and run at least 2 joint account-mapping or customer sessions. Avoid signing partners who cannot name target customers or commit a next action.

### Day-60 exit criteria

- 3 active pilots/evaluations cumulative, with at least 1 paid or in commercial approval.
- 18 qualified discovery calls cumulative and a visible pipeline of at least 3x the near-term bookings target.
- Two acquisition channels show repeatable activity: founder-led outbound plus either referrals, partner sourcing, or events/community.
- Four partners have a named target account and scheduled next action.
- One approved proof asset and one quantified pilot result.

## 5. Phase 3 — Days 61–90: close, reference, and prepare a repeatable engine

### Objectives

- Close the first repeatable customer segment.
- Turn successful pilots into references, referrals, and expansion.
- Improve conversion and onboarding before increasing volume.
- Decide what to scale, automate, or stop.

### Closing and expansion

- Focus all active opportunities on a defined close plan with economic buyer, procurement steps, security review, legal terms, and decision date.
- Offer simple commercial packages rather than custom pricing for every prospect:
  - **Pilot:** fixed scope and fixed duration;
  - **Team:** one or two cloud environments, core posture and workflow;
  - **Enterprise:** multiple environments, expanded connectors, governance, support, and procurement requirements.
- Price around monitored scope and business value, not number of alerts. Keep the first paid pilot easy to approve and credit it toward an annual contract where appropriate.
- Ask successful users for three things separately: a quote, a referral, and permission to use an anonymized result.
- Run an expansion review for every customer: additional cloud, SBOM/vulnerability workflow, more business units, or governance/reporting.

### Proof and authority

- Publish one concise case study with the problem, scope, evidence discovered, business impact, and implementation effort.
- Host a small practitioner session with a customer, partner, or independent security expert; keep it educational and avoid product-heavy promotion.
- Create a buyer-facing comparison page explaining the difference between CSPM, vulnerability management, AI governance, and ScoutGrid's evidence-backed AI posture/exposure workflow.
- Build a reference library of answers to the top 20 objections from calls.

### Operating system

- Review funnel metrics every Monday and customer/pilot health every Friday.
- Track source, segment, trigger, persona, message, stage conversion, time-to-value, pilot-to-paid conversion, and reasons lost.
- Reserve one day per week for product feedback and onboarding friction; do not let every prospect create a custom roadmap.
- Create lightweight automation only after a manual process repeats three times: CRM reminders, meeting follow-ups, demo environment reset, pilot checklist, and reporting.

### Day-90 exit criteria

- 2–4 paying customers or paid pilots, depending on sales-cycle length.
- At least 2 referenceable outcomes or written testimonials.
- One segment/message combination clearly outperforming the rest.
- A qualified pipeline at least 3x the next-quarter bookings target.
- Six active partners, with at least two producing or co-selling opportunities.
- Documented sales script, discovery questions, pilot plan, onboarding checklist, security FAQ, pricing, and case study.
- A clear next-quarter decision: scale the wedge, revise it, or stop pursuing it.

## 7. Core messaging and talk tracks

### One-sentence version

ScoutGrid helps security teams discover and prioritize AI-system posture and exposure risk across AWS and Azure using read-only, evidence-backed analysis, then route the resulting work into accountable remediation workflows.

### Discovery questions

1. How do you currently know which AI systems, models, agents, tools, and data connections exist?
2. Which AI-security questions are hardest to answer with evidence today?
3. What happens when an AI asset has no clear owner?
4. Which upcoming customer, audit, board, or production milestone creates urgency?
5. What would a successful two-to-four-week assessment need to prove?

### Objection handling

- **“We already have CSPM.”** ScoutGrid can complement cloud posture tooling by organizing AI-specific inventory, evidence, policy findings, and exposure relationships.
- **“We already have vulnerability management.”** ScoutGrid can connect AI-system context and ownership to the existing remediation workflow; the wedge is AI posture and exposure, with vulnerability expansion later.
- **“We cannot share prompts or sensitive data.”** The initial motion is based on read-only metadata/evidence boundaries; validate the exact data flow during the security review.
- **“We are too early.”** Offer a time-boxed inventory/readiness baseline so the buyer can understand current coverage before committing to a larger program.

## 8. Measurement dashboard

Track weekly:

- new target accounts and contacts;
- touches, reply rate, positive reply rate, meetings booked;
- discovery-to-qualified and qualified-to-pilot conversion;
- pilot start rate, time to first value, pilot-to-paid conversion;
- pipeline value and stage aging;
- partner meetings, sourced opportunities, and influenced pipeline;
- content engagement only when it leads to conversations;
- top objections, lost reasons, and product gaps.

Use these decision rules:

- Low replies: change segment, trigger, or message before increasing volume.
- Replies but few meetings: improve CTA and qualification.
- Meetings but no pilots: sharpen urgency, proof, or pilot scope.
- Pilots but no paid conversion: validate buyer, success metric, pricing, and procurement earlier.
- Partner meetings without named opportunities: stop broad partner recruiting and focus on direct referrals.

## 9. What not to do in the first 90 days

- Do not market every ScoutGrid feature at once.
- Do not spend heavily on paid acquisition, conferences, or broad brand campaigns before message-market evidence exists.
- Do not build a large partner program or extensive integrations before partners produce customer conversations.
- Do not give free pilots with undefined scope or no decision date.
- Do not let framework mappings substitute for measurable customer value.
- Do not count downloads, impressions, or partner logos as traction unless they create qualified conversations or revenue.
